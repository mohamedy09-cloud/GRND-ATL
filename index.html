Strategic Overview

GRND is positioned as a serious specialty coffee concept in Atlanta. This plan assumes a mid-volume, single-location brick-and-mortar format — targeting 150–400 drinks per day. The espresso bar is the core capital investment. Smart purchasing means treating the espresso machine, grinders, water filtration, and refrigeration as long-horizon infrastructure, while buying smallwares and consumables lean at open and restocking as volume grows.

Key Assumptions — Flag & Confirm

This plan assumes: 1) You are serving espresso-based drinks, batch drip, and cold beverages. 2) You will offer light pastry/food from an outside commissary — no full kitchen required. 3) Your space already has or will have 200–240V electrical service, a grease trap is not required (confirm with Fulton County Health — espresso bars often qualify for a coffee shop exception), and floor drains exist or can be added. 4) You will apply for a Georgia food service permit through Fulton County Environmental Health. 5) Space is approximately 800–1,800 sq ft.

Atlanta-Specific Notes

Fulton County Environmental Health governs food service permits in most Atlanta addresses. Plan for a pre-opening inspection. NSF-rated equipment is often required for commercial permits — this plan flags that where relevant. Hoshizaki America is headquartered in Peachtree City, GA — strong local service network and parts availability for ice machines. La Marzocco, Nuova Simonelli, and Synesso all have certified service technicians active in the Atlanta metro market. Creation Commercial (based in Atlanta area) and Specialty Coffee Association–affiliated dealers in GA are your primary local sourcing contacts for espresso equipment.

G Buy New vs Buy Pre-Owned — Strategy Guide
Always Buy New
  • Water filtration system — Filters must be fresh; unknown history compromises your machine immediately.
  • 3-compartment sink — Low cost to buy new, must pass inspection, no point in pre-owned.
  • Hand sinks — Same logic. Buy new, install correctly.
  • POS hardware — Technology cycles fast; pre-owned iPad stands may lack compatibility.
  • Floor mats, smallwares, consumables — These are hygiene items. Always new.
  • Bar scales, thermometers — Calibration reliability matters. Buy new.
  • Espresso grinders (primary) — Burr wear is invisible; unknown grinders need new burrs anyway. Buy new unless grinder is certified reconditioned with new burrs from a reputable dealer.
Can Safely Buy Pre-Owned
  • Espresso machine — ONLY from certified commercial dealer with written warranty, service history, and boiler inspection. La Marzocco Linea Classic is the safest bet.
  • Refrigeration (True, Beverage Air, Hoshizaki) — Commercial units last 15–20 years. OK to buy pre-owned if compressor is warranted and unit is inspected.
  • Metro shelving — Simple stainless steel; no moving parts. Pre-owned is fine if NSF-rated.
  • Sheet pans, hotel pans, storage containers — OK pre-owned if sanitized; commercial-grade is commercial-grade.
  • Batch brewer (FETCO/BUNN) — OK from certified dealer with warranty and descaled/cleaned documentation.
  • Airpots / servers — Buy new. They're inexpensive and seals degrade.
Ice Machine Warning

Pre-owned ice machines are high-risk. Ice machines have complex water circuits that build scale, biofilm, and mineral deposits. Unless a pre-owned machine comes with a full cleaning/descale service documentation, certified replacement parts, and a written dealer warranty — buy new. Hoshizaki's Atlanta-area service network makes a new Hoshizaki the smart play here.
